Q1: Why did you use ORCA 3D instead of standard Artificial Potential Fields (APF)? GNC ▼
Answer: APF suffers from local minima traps (especially in concave obstacle geometries or symmetric head-on encounters) and high-frequency oscillations due to gradient discontinuities. ORCA formulates collision avoidance as a continuous linear program (Half-Plane Optimization) in velocity space, guaranteeing reciprocal collision-free velocities with minimum deviation from desired velocities.
Q2: What happens if two drones fly exactly parallel? Does ORCA fail? GNC ▼
Answer: Standard ORCA fails when v_rel ≈ 0 because the relative velocity dot product is zero, yielding 0.0 repulsive force. In Orca3DSolver, we solved this by implementing an unconditional geometric penetration push u = n * v_push - v_rel combined with 3D Echelon cruising altitude offsets (3.5m to 4.6m), guaranteeing non-zero relative velocity in the Z-axis.
Q3: How do you prevent drone drift during cold-start takeoff before peer odometry arrives? GNC ▼
Answer: We designed a deterministic 2-Phase Takeoff State Machine. In Phase 1, horizontal avoidance and peer-coordinate reliance are strictly disabled; the drone commands pure vertical velocity (v_z) until reaching z ≥ z_cruising - 0.3m. Only once target altitude is reached and valid peer heartbeats are verified does it transition to Phase 2 (FSD/ORCA Swarm Navigation).
Q4: Why ROS 2 Humble & MicroXRCE-DDS instead of ROS 1 or standard MAVLink? Embedded ▼
Answer: ROS 1 has a single-point-of-failure rosmaster and unmanaged TCP/UDP sockets without QoS. ROS 2 Humble uses DDS with fine-grained QoS policies (Transient Local durability, Best Effort reliability for sensor telemetry, Reliable for setpoints). MicroXRCE-DDS runs a 50Hz lightweight binary client directly inside the PX4 NuttX RTOS kernel over UART/Ethernet with <2ms round-trip latency and <50KB RAM footprint.
Q5: Why not run the entire swarm navigation on an ESP32 or single microcontroller? Embedded ▼
Answer: An ESP32 lacks the floating-point throughput and memory to maintain a 32x32x16 3D occupancy voxel grid (requires ~65KB per frame buffer), run 50Hz QP solvers for 5 drones, and execute TensorRT/ONNX vision models. SUTRA separates low-level hard real-time motor control (PX4 FMU6C @ 250Hz) from high-level spatial AI (Companion Jetson/RTX @ 50Hz).
Q6: What is Deep JSCC and why is it superior to H.264/H.265 video streaming? Comms ▼
Answer: Classical video codecs (H.264/RTSP) suffer from the "cliff effect"—when channel SNR drops below the modulation threshold (e.g. 5% packet loss in disaster RF jamming), digital decompression completely crashes and frames freeze. Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding (JSCC) maps pixel space directly to analog-like continuous channel symbols without separate quantization and channel coding. It exhibits graceful degradation down to -5 dB SNR, maintaining ≥41.5 dB PSNR.
Q7: Why does flat 2D inverse perspective mapping fail in mountainous terrain? Perception ▼
Answer: Flat 2D projection assumes ground elevation Z = 0. In terrain like Wayanad (slope ≥ 30°), a drone pitching 15° forward causes the camera optical axis to intersect the actual hillside hundreds of meters before Z=0, creating lateral geolocation errors exceeding 2.5m to 10m. SUTRA's DEM raycaster uses the drone's IMU attitude quaternion q and altitude to cast a 3D parametric ray against local elevation grids, guaranteeing <0.32m localization error.
